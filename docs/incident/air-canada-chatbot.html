Initial AccessCustomer query
A grieving customer used Air Canada's public website chatbot to ask about bereavement fares; no adversary or injected payload was involved — the harm came from the model's own output.
Jake Moffatt used Air Canada's website chatbot on November 11, 2022, to inquire about bereavement fares after a family member's death. fosterandcompany.com, case background

Action on Obj.Negligent misrepresentation
The chatbot fabricated a bereavement-refund policy that contradicted Air Canada's actual terms. On 2024-02-14 the BC Civil Resolution Tribunal held the airline liable for negligent misrepresentation (2024 BCCRT 149), rejecting the argument that the bot was a separate entity.
While a chatbot has an interactive component, it is still just a part of Air Canada's website. Tribunal Member Rivers, Moffatt v. Air Canada 2024 BCCRT 149
